# Research: voice

## Summary
No current video model reliably keeps an invented voice from one clip to the next by itself. In November 2026 the two dependable ways to get the same French voice in every shot are: give the video model the same short voice sample each time, and/or convert the audio afterwards with a voice changer, which keeps the timing so the lips stay in sync.

Route 1 (speech generated by the video model): Seedance 2.0/2.5 accept a voice sample and claim French; Kling 3.0 binds a voice to a character but its official language list has no French; Veo 3.1 has no voice lock apart from preset voices for Ultra subscribers; Sora is shut down. Route 2 (voice changer): ElevenLabs converts about 10 minutes a month free and keeps pacing and emotion; Higgsfield has a Change Voice tool. Route 3 (line recorded first, video driven by it): the most control over French; Wan 2.7, OmniHuman 2.0 and Higgsfield Intone keep body acting, Kling Avatar and Hedra lean towards talking heads; roughly 1.70 to 3.50 USD per 30 s. Route 4 (silent acting, loose dub): no extra cost, no lip-sync.

Best value: one speaker and one short line per shot, generated with Seedance plus the character's voice sample, then a voice changer only if the voice drifts. On Higgsfield, Seedance 2.0 Mini at 480p quotes 0.5 credit per second (15 credits per 30 s per take), so the 10 free credits cover about 20 s of tests, not a whole sketch. Fallback: record the line with a TTS and drive the shot with that audio.

Limits: page fetching was blocked (invideo.io, lumalabs.ai, dreamina.capcut.com, kling.ai are outside the session's allowed network) and the web-search budget ran out, so French quality, free open-source tools and several prices rest on search summaries only.

## Findings
- **Native voices drift between generations; cross-clip voice consistency needs an explicit voice input (sample, bound voice or preset).** [medium]
  Veo 3.1: a user review reports no voice consistency across generations even with a fixed seed. Google Flow later added experimental preset 'Voice ingredients' (Veo 3.1 Fast, Ultra plan, character image required, voices do not carry over with Extend) and preset-plus-prompt 'custom voices' in Veo 3.1 Lite, region-limited. No source shows uploading your own voice sample to Veo. An Aug 2026 roundup says most frontier models now produce sound and that voice control is where they differ.
- **Seedance 2.0 and 2.5 accept voice/audio references and are the best-documented route for a recurring voice, but the output voice is guided, not copied.** [medium]
  Seedance 2.5: up to 10 reference audio files, 30 s combined, must be paired with an image or video reference; clips up to 30 s. Seedance 2.0: up to 3 audio clips of 2-15 s. Guides advise one approved clip per character reused in every shot ('Mara from @Image 1, voiced by @Audio 1'), fixed speaker names and wording, one speaker / one line / one shot, 10-15 s of dialogue rather than 30 s. Sources conflict: OpenArt says a ~15 s sample works as voice cloning; seedance2pro and others say the audio is a structural reference (rhythm, timing, lip-sync) and the voice is newly generated. One test: 'guided the rhythm, timing and voice, but the output isn't a copy'. In a Curious Refuge comparison Seedance 2.0 had the best lip-sync among prompt-to-speech video models; Kling looked good but less natural.
- **French in Seedance is claimed and demoed but not independently tested; Kling 3.0 native audio does not list French.** [medium]
  Seedance 2.5: vendors list 10+ or 11 languages including French, and ByteDance demos include a French exchange; the model pages have no language setting. Guides say to write 'Dialogue language: French from France', put the line in French inside double quotes, and test one short line at 480p first. Reported pitfalls: accents, fast delivery, background music and ambiguous speaker labels destabilise timing; one reviewer says lines over about 5 words often desync; another that sync drops past 10 s; multi-person lip-sync is an acknowledged weak point. Kling Video 3.0 / 3.0 Omni native audio: Chinese, English, Japanese, Korean, Spanish only (Kling's own pages); voice binding to a character element uses a 5-30 s sample (other guides say 3-8 s).
- **Google's Gemini Omni Flash takes voice references in Google's own apps, but that input is not generally exposed elsewhere and voice continuity was reported as regressed.** [medium]
  Launched May 2026 (I/O); at launch audio input was limited to voice references; clips about 10 s. A developer forum thread asks for allowlisting of audio references on gemini-omni-1.2-flash, and another user reports the 1.1 Flash update broke clip-to-clip voice continuity in Flow. On Higgsfield, Gemini Omni Flash 1.1 and 1.2 expose no audio input. Sora 2 is no longer an option: shutdown announced 24 Mar 2026, app closed 26 Apr 2026, API scheduled to end 24 Sep 2026.
- **Live Higgsfield figures (read today from the account's own catalog and cost quotes, nothing generated): 10 credits, free plan; Seedance 2.0 Mini is the cheapest talking model at 0.5 credit/s.** [high]
  Quotes for 10 s vertical clips: Seedance 2.0 Mini 480p 5 credits (2.5 for 5 s), 720p 10; Seedance 2.0 fast 480p 10; Seedance 2.5 Flash 480p 20; Seedance 2.5 480p 30; Wan 2.7 720p 15; Kling 3.0 std 20 with sound, 15 silent; Grok Imagine 1.5 Lite 480p 10; MiniMax H3 Max 480p 15; MAGI-2 1080p 25; Gemini Omni Flash 1.2 720p 22.5 (360p 8 s = 6); Veo 3.1 Fast 8 s = 32. One French line of about 140 characters: Qwen TTS Flash 0.06 credit (French supported), ElevenLabs engine 0.45, Eleven v4 0.69, Seed Audio 1.0 1 credit. Models with an audio-reference input: Seedance 2.0 / 2.0 Mini / 2.5 / 2.5 Flash, Wan 2.7, MiniMax H3, MAGI-2, Grok Video 1.5, and Higgsfield Intone. Seedance supports 4:3 and 9:16 natively. A credit is worth about 0.05 USD (Higgsfield blog: 40 credits = 2.00 USD; Plus 49 USD / 1,000 credits). Plan gating is not checked by a quote: the catalog says Seedance 2.0 'fast' needs Basic/Starter and 'std' needs Plus; third-party pages say Starter (19 USD) covers Seedance 2.0 Fast and Mini.
- **Route 1 cost per 30 s of dialogue ranges from about 0.75 USD to about 14 USD per take depending on model and resolution.** [medium]
  Higgsfield quotes scaled to 30 s: Seedance 2.0 Mini 480p 15 credits (~0.75 USD), 720p 30 credits (~1.50 USD); Seedance 2.5 480p 90 credits (~4.50 USD); Kling 3.0 std with sound 60 credits (~3 USD); Veo 3.1 Fast ~120 credits (~6 USD). Seedance 2.5 by API: OpenRouter 0.103 USD/s at 480p and 0.231 USD/s at 720p (3.09 / 6.93 USD per 30 s); fal 0.2205 USD/s at 480p and 0.473 USD/s at 720p (6.6 / 14.2 USD). Dreamina lists Seedance 2.5 at 37 credits/s against a reported 120 free daily credits (region-dependent), so free Dreamina credits cover about 3 s. Budget 2-3 takes per shot: the multiplication is mine, not from a source.
- **Route 2: a speech-to-speech voice changer gives an identical voice in every shot and keeps lip-sync, at almost no cost.** [medium]
  ElevenLabs Voice Changer keeps the original intonation, pacing and emotional delivery and only swaps the timbre, so mouth timing still matches. Cost: about 1,000 credits per minute; free plan 10,000 credits a month = about 10 minutes (30 s = 500 credits), non-commercial with attribution, credits shared with TTS; API 0.12 USD/min (0.06 USD per 30 s); Starter 6 USD/month. Limits: 5 minutes and 50 MB per conversion. Higgsfield 'Change Voice' swaps the voice in an existing video ('new voice, same delivery and timing', visuals untouched) with a preset or custom voice; its credit price was not found; cloning a custom voice is listed at 40 credits (~2 USD). Pitfalls (my inference, not sourced): the changer keeps whatever accent or mispronunciation the video model produced, music and sound effects under the voice get mangled, and two speakers in one clip must be converted separately.
- **Route 3: recording the line first and driving the video with it gives the most control over French; full-body acting and camera survive mainly with general video models and OmniHuman 2.0.** [medium]
  Wan 2.7 image-to-video takes a driving audio (WAV/MP3, 2-30 s, max 15 MB); the audio sets the clip length and drives lip-sync and motion timing; 0.05-0.15 USD/s by API; Higgsfield quote 15 credits per 10 s at 720p (~2.25 USD per 30 s). OmniHuman 2.0 (BytePlus, 15 Oct 2026): face, head and body from audio, optional prompt for actions, scene and camera, handles cartoon and pixel characters, 0.07 USD/s (2.10 USD per 30 s); not confirmed on Dreamina. Kling Avatar 2.0: image + audio, up to 5 minutes, human or cartoon, 0.0562 USD/s standard and 0.115 USD/s pro on fal (1.69 / 3.45 USD per 30 s); described as talking-avatar output, and one test says it adds unwanted visual detail. Hedra Character-3: 6-8 credits/s, Basic 15 USD / 1,500 credits (about 1.80-2.40 USD per 30 s), free tier disputed (100-400 watermarked credits), 'can get unpredictable'. Sync.so re-syncs an existing video: lipsync-2 0.04-0.05 USD/s, lipsync-2-pro 0.067-0.083, sync-3 0.107-0.133 (sync-3 is the one aimed at animation and closed lips). Higgsfield Intone: 1-2 characters speak 1-6 recordings, 2-30 s, staged in up to five shots, 9:16 or 4:3; price not quotable without audio. Avatar tools in general: lips fine, hands and body awkward.
- **A TTS-first variant with Seedance exists (Seed Audio line fed as reference), but sources disagree on whether Seedance plays your audio back or regenerates it.** [low]
  MindStudio and Atlas Cloud describe generating each shot's audio with Seed Audio 1.0 (which can clone from a reference sample) and handing it to Seedance as a reference, with prompts like '@Image1 is the character speaking. Lip-sync dialogue to @Audio1' and the transcript repeated in the prompt. Other guides say Seedance generates new audio matching the pacing. No source confirms word-for-word preservation, so the final voice may still need a voice-changer pass.
- **French TTS pitfalls: write numbers in words, use a French-native voice, and expect voice drift between separate Gemini TTS requests.** [medium]
  ElevenLabs: digits and symbols are ambiguous across languages, so spell numbers out or set text normalisation with the language; an English-native voice speaking French keeps an English accent; v3 accepts IPA in slashes (80-90% consistency by their figure) and has no SSML pauses (use dashes or ellipses). ElevenLabs free plan: 10,000 characters a month (~10 minutes), non-commercial. Gemini 3.1 Flash TTS: free in AI Studio (preview, free-tier data may be used by Google), paid 20 USD per million audio tokens (third-party estimate ~0.03 USD/min); developer forum threads report voice identity changing between separate requests and accent drift (thread titles only, not read). Nothing was found on liaison specifically.
- **Route 4 (silent acting plus loose dub) is the cheapest and simplest but was not sourced; it is a framing choice, not a lip-sync method.** [low]
  Silent generation costs less (Higgsfield quote: Kling 3.0 std 10 s is 15 credits silent against 20 with sound) and the TTS can be free. No source was found on how the reference accounts handle it; the searches on the PS2 trend only covered still images and generic image-to-video. My own assessment: it works on shots where the mouth is small, turned away or off-screen (listener reactions, over-the-shoulder, wide shots) and looks wrong on close-ups, which the reference style relies on.

## Recommendation
Primary route (best quality for effort): native speech with a fixed voice sample, locked afterwards with a voice changer only where needed.
1. Fix each character's voice once: a clean 8-15 s French sample per character, no music. Keep the two files and reuse them unchanged.
2. Write the sketch as one speaker and one short line per shot (aim for under 8-10 s and about 15 words), cutting between the two characters rather than having both talk in one clip.
3. Generate each shot with Seedance in reference mode: character image + that character's voice sample as audio reference, prompt stating 'French from France', the line in French inside double quotes, plus acting and camera directions. Seedance 2.0 Mini at 480p is the cheapest on Higgsfield (0.5 credit/s, 4:3 supported); Seedance 2.5 is the quality option at 3 credits/s.
4. If a voice drifts between shots, pass that clip's audio through a speech-to-speech changer to the same target voice (ElevenLabs free plan, about 10 minutes a month, or Higgsfield Change Voice). Timing is unchanged, so lip-sync holds. Keep music and sound effects out of the generation and add them in the edit.
Cost: about 15 credits (~0.75 USD) per 30 s per take at 480p, realistically 45 credits (~2.25 USD) with retakes; the voice step is free. The 10 free credits buy about 20 s of tests, so a full 30-60 s sketch needs either a small credit purchase or a plan.

First test before committing (2.5 credits): one 5 s, 480p Seedance 2.0 Mini shot with one French line and the voice sample. It answers three unknowns at once: whether the free plan can run the model, whether the French is clean, and how close the voice lands.

Fallback: if the model's French is wrong (accent, a misread number, a swallowed word), record the line with a TTS first, where spelling and pronunciation are fully controllable, and drive the shot with that audio: Wan 2.7 or Higgsfield Intone on Higgsfield, or OmniHuman 2.0 at 0.07 USD/s. Last resort for wide or action shots: silent acting with the TTS line laid over, placed on shots where the mouth is not readable.

Avoid for French dialogue: Kling 3.0 native audio (French not in its language list) and Veo 3.1 (no voice lock, about 6 USD per 30 s on Higgsfield).
