# Research: trend

## Summary
Everything I found describes the same two-stage AI recipe, which fits the earlier conclusion that these are not hand-made 3D. First, a still of each character and set is made in a "PS2/PS3 cutscene" render style with an image model (sources name Nano Banana 2.1, Seedream 5.5, and ChatGPT or Gemini image tools). Second, those stills go to a video model that generates motion, voice and lip-sync in one pass, in clips of about 5-15 seconds. The clips are then cut together and captioned in an editor such as CapCut.

I found no public statement from @zak.kho or @3dmoggerr about their tools. The nearest direct evidence is a similar creator (3dMarl0n, who sells a "PS2 style" course) crediting Flashloop.ai, an aggregator offering Seedance 2.0/2.5, Veo, Kling, Sora and Grok. A YouTube Short describes the method as "3 images, 1 prompt" (character, place, background person), which is how Seedance's multi-reference mode works. The 4:3 frame also fits: Seedance and Grok Imagine offer 4:3, while Veo 3.1, Sora 2 and Kling 3.0 do not. The exact 768x576 size matches no generator output I could find and is probably a re-encode.

Best-supported toolchain: image-model stills, then Seedance 2.0 (Fast/Mini) or 2.5 with native audio through an aggregator, edited in CapCut. Confidence is medium for Seedance specifically and high for the overall image-then-video-with-native-audio method.

Two limits. This session could not open any page (YouTube, Atlas Cloud and others were blocked by the network allowlist), so every fact comes from search-result summaries; quoted prompts and prices need rechecking. Several model names (Seedance 2.5, Kling 4.0, Gemini Omni Flash, Nano Banana 2.1) post-date my own knowledge and are reported as the sources state them.

Budget: Higgsfield's free plan reportedly includes no video models, so the 10 credits will not make this sketch.

## Findings
- **No public statement of tools was found for @zak.kho or @3dmoggerr.** [high]
  Searches on both handles, on 'no camera no actors' and on '3D animations' gym-skit accounts returned nothing about their production method. Any attribution of a specific generator to these two accounts is inference from the wider trend, not evidence.
- **The closest direct creator evidence points to an aggregator whose main models are Seedance: 3dMarl0n credits Flashloop.ai.** [medium]
  3dMarl0n (TikTok @americandante) posts PS2-style AI 'movies' ('Don't Be the Friend, Be the Mogger'), tags #ps2 with 'Tutorial in bio' (about 134K likes on one post) and sells 'The 3DMarl0n method' at 3dmarlon.com. One post caption reads 'AI edit using Flashloop.ai'. Flashloop hosts Seedance 2.0, 2.0 Mini and 2.5 plus Veo, Kling, Sora and Grok, so the exact model is still not stated. On Flashloop, Seedance 2.0 takes up to 7 reference images or a first and last frame, clips up to 15 s, Fast mode at 480p/720p; Seedance 2.5 is listed with up to 30 s, up to 50 references and native audio. The course page itself does not name its tools in the indexed text.
- **The 2026 'PS2 cutscene' AI video wave is dated to 3 September 2026, and tutorials describe an image-then-video recipe.** [medium]
  A template site says the wave began with @santeluca (Luca Maxim), a 16-second PS2-style cutscene, 3.9 million views and 462,900 likes by 16 November 2026; this is a marketing page and not independently confirmed. It says TikTok tutorials 'typically combine a chatbot that turns your selfie into a PS2-era game character sheet, then a video tool that animates it, and you add the subtitle and the song in an editor'. Its own template uses Nano Banana 2.1 to place the person in the scene, then an image-to-video model with original sound, output 10 s; other templates on the same site name Seedance 2.5. YouTube tutorials exist: 'The Secret Behind THOSE Viral AI PS2 Videos (Full Tutorial)' uploaded 2026-09-12 (a commenter says about 10 s costs 30-60 credits) and 'How I Make PS2-Style AI Animation Videos' (cites creator R11MURU). I could not open YouTube, so the tools shown on screen are unconfirmed.
- **A YouTube Short describes the method as '3 images, 1 prompt', which is reference-to-video rather than a single start frame.** [medium]
  The Short 'How to make the PS2-style AI videos (3 images, 1 prompt)' is summarized as: 'One image for the character, one for the place, one for the guy in the background.' That matches Seedance's multi-reference mode: Seedance 2.0 accepts up to 12 references (9 images, 3 videos, 3 audio clips) addressed as @Image1, @Image2; Seedance 2.5 accepts up to 50 (30 images, 10 videos, 10 audio). The Short's tool name was not visible in search results.
- **A vendor guide (Nov 2026) gives the two-step PS2 workflow and names Omni 1.1 Flash, Kling 4.0 and MiniMax H3 as animation engines.** [medium]
  Atlas Cloud: turn a portrait into a low-poly PS2 character with an image model, verify it, then upload it as the 'locked first frame inside a retro game cutscene AI engine such as Omni 1.1 Flash, Kling 4.0, or Minimax h3'. It then suggests adding CRT artifacts, pixelated HUD graphics and compressed console audio in post. This is a sales blog, so the model list reflects what the vendor sells. It does not cover dialogue or lip-sync in the text I could see.
- **Step-by-step workflow that the sources agree on: stills first, one short clip per line, assemble in an editor.** [medium]
  1) Character sheet per person (front, 3/4, full body, same outfit) and a set image, in the target render style. 2) Approve one still per shot before animating. 3) Generate each shot with the stills as references or first frame, one small action and one camera move per shot, the spoken line in the prompt so voice and lip-sync come from the same pass. 4) Clip length: Seedance 2.0 makes 4-15 s, Seedance 2.5 up to 30 s at 480p/720p, 24 fps; Kling 3.0 up to 15 s; Veo 3.1 capped at 8 s. 5) A finished piece is 6-20 scenes assembled in an editor (CapCut is the one named), with captions and music added there. A common stack named for '3D animation' pieces is Higgsfield Soul Cinema or Nano Banana 2 for stills, Seedance 2.0 for video, CapCut for the edit.
- **Example prompt wording for the PS2 look (as surfaced by search summaries; recheck against the pages).** [medium]
  Video (Atlas Cloud): 'An [DURATION]-second story-driven cutscene from an early-2000s PS2 game, starting with the exact character and scene from the reference image. Maintain authentic early-2000s PS2 cutscene graphics, low-resolution textures, polygonal geometry, and simple lighting.' plus a line forbidding photorealism and character morphing. Image (Atlas Cloud): a 'PlayStation 2 in-engine 3D cutscene portrait' with a 2001-2004 console look. Most-shared tutorial prompt (dated 23 Oct 2026, per makeviral): a 'recognizable PS2-era low-poly 3D video game character with visible polygons and low-resolution textures', 'not anime, cartoon, or modern ultra-realistic 3D'; also 'visible polygons, low-resolution textures, gritty action-game atmosphere'. Media.io: open with 'PS2-era 3D game screenshot' or 'PS2 cutscene', then scene details; negative terms 'ultra-detailed skin, ray tracing, HDR glow'. Seedance cutscene structure (X post): name the look first ('...video game cutscene...') then timed beats '0-4s: ...', '4-8s: ...'. I found no published prompt for the more realistic 'PS3-era' variant.
- **Dialogue prompting: quoted line, named speaker, language and accent stated; one speaker per shot; short lines. French works but is weaker than English or Mandarin.** [medium]
  Format: speaker, language/accent, tone, timing, then the line in double quotes in its native script, e.g. 'The woman says quietly in French, "On rentre ?"'. A Seedance prompting skill says French lip-sync is 'partly supported' and advises announcing 'Dialogue language: French from France' before the line. Guides say one speaker per clip syncs best; for two speakers label each character and line, or split into one-speaker shots. Line-length rules of thumb disagree: about 12 words per 10 s clip and 20 per 15 s in one guide, 'more than 5 words almost always desynchronizes' in another. Front or three-quarter framing helps the mouth track. Add 'no music' (or 'no BGM; generate only environmental sounds and action sounds') so the dialogue track stays clean for editing.
- **Voice changes between shots unless an audio reference is attached; this is the main failure mode and has a known fix.** [medium]
  Without a voice reference the model invents a voice per generation and can add an unintended accent. Fix: create one clean, single-speaker sample per character and attach it as an audio reference in every shot, repeating the same speaker label and voice description. One guide says 6 s 'drifts by the second cut' while about 30 s holds; Seedance 2.5 reportedly accepts up to 30 s of reference audio in total. For non-English pronunciation, generate the line with text-to-speech and attach it so the model lip-syncs to that audio (reported to work on Seedance 2.0 and 2.5). Fallback: post-dub or re-lip. Timbre is described as 'not locked' even with a reference, so expect retries.
- **Garbled text is avoided by never asking the model for text: captions are added in the editor.** [medium]
  Guides say to generate a text-free plate and typeset on an editable layer. To stop spontaneous subtitles: write 'subtitle-free' or 'no subtitles' (an instruction, not a guarantee), remove any text from reference images, and note that spurious subtitles are reported as much rarer in landscape than in portrait. Seedance 2.0 has no negative-prompt field, so constraints must be phrased positively. ByteDance itself acknowledged text rendering as a weak point at the February 2026 launch.
- **Face drift and loss of the retro look have specific causes and fixes.** [medium]
  Face drift: use a front and a profile reference at good resolution, at most two characters per shot with clear spatial separation, and shorter clips; Seedance 2.5 reviews mention occasional morphing. Style drift: image models (FLUX, Seedream) tend to read early-3D console graphics as modern vector art, and the video model's temporal smoothing erases hard polygon edges, so the style line must be repeated in every video prompt ('no photorealism'). Cutting to a different aesthetic inside one generation can make the model lose the style. Post tricks: downscale then nearest-neighbor upscale, CRT/scanline overlay, compressed audio.
- **The 4:3 ratio narrows the field to Seedance or Grok Imagine; the exact 768x576 size is not a generator fingerprint.** [medium]
  Seedance 2.x lists ratios 21:9, 16:9, 4:3, 1:1, 3:4, 9:16; Grok Imagine's API lists 4:3 too. Veo 3.1 and Sora 2 offer only 16:9 and 9:16; Kling 3.0 offers 16:9, 9:16 and 1:1; Gemini Omni Flash is reported inconsistently. Seedance's reported 4:3 sizes are 752x560 at 480p and 1112x834 at 720p (third-party pages), not 768x576. My inference: 768x576 is a platform or editor re-encode. TikTok commonly serves a 576-pixel rendition (576x1024 for vertical), and 768x576 is the 4:3 equivalent, which would mean the creators upload the 4:3 clip as is and TikTok letterboxes it. No source confirms this directly.
- **Budget facts: Higgsfield's free plan reportedly has no video; the cheapest Seedance tier costs about 0.04 USD per second.** [low]
  Higgsfield's help center says the free plan has no monthly or daily credit allowance, and November 2026 guides say the free tier opens selected image models only. Seedance 2.0 Fast and Mini sit on the lowest paid plan (reported variously as 9 USD/120 credits, 15 USD/200 credits or 19 USD/270 credits per month); Seedance 2.5 needs Plus. Reported Higgsfield costs: Seedance 2.0 Mini 480p 8 credits per 8 s clip (about 0.60 USD); Seedance 2.0 5 s at 480p 15 credits; Seedance 2.5 10 s at 480p 30 credits (1.47 USD), at 720p 70 credits (3.43 USD). BytePlus API: 2.0 Mini from about 0.04 USD/s at 480p, Mini 720p 0.08, Fast 720p 0.12, Seedance 2.5 0.10 at 480p and 0.23 at 720p. Dreamina free tier: 120 credits a day for US accounts (other regions vary), about 24 s of 2.0 Mini or 15 s of 2.0 Fast per day, watermarked, non-commercial. Grok Imagine video is reported as paid-only since March 2026. Sources conflict, so check live pricing.

## Recommendation
Plan the remake as an AI image-then-video pipeline, not 3D.

1. Make one approved reference still per character (front and three-quarter view, same outfit) and one of the workshop, in a "PS2/PS3 in-engine cutscene" style, with no text anywhere in the images.
2. Generate one 5-8 s clip per line with a Seedance 2.0 Mini or Fast class model at 480p in 4:3. Keep one speaker per shot, put the French line in quotes with "says in French (France)", and add "no subtitles, no music". The low resolution suits the look and is the cheapest tier.
3. Lock each voice by attaching the same clean voice sample (or a text-to-speech recording of the line) as audio reference in every shot.
4. Assemble in CapCut and add the captions there, never in the generation.

Before paying, test two or three shots on a free daily-credit tier (Dreamina, if it is available in France) and check on Higgsfield's live pricing page what the 10 credits actually allow; the sources say the free plan has no video. For a 45 s sketch, expect roughly 45-150 credits, or about 2-6 USD of API time, including retries. That range is my own estimate from the per-second prices found, not a quoted figure.

To raise confidence on the exact tool, have someone watch the two YouTube tutorials (xI51f2FFbpk and 4MUN4YvCGpY) and open 3dmarlon.com, which this session could not reach.
